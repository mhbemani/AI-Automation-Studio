# Agent brief: "car-audio" — voices, timing and the shot timeline for the car scene

Context: the studio is building a short 3D scene (a human and Claude talking in a car). A separate agent is fixing the Blender scene in parallel; **you do not use Blender at all**. Your job is the audio and the machine-readable timeline that the later animation/render step will consume. Read `lab/BRIEF.md` first (rules: $0, minimal, ask before large downloads, never `pkill` processes you did not start), then `lab/results/tts.md` and `lab/results/performance.md` (how Kokoro, Whisper and timing worked before), and `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/production/dialogue_v1.json` (the made-up dialogue, voices, shot rules; the characters, voices and content are placeholders for a first end-to-end sample). Work in `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/production/`. Machine: Apple M2, 8 GB; run heavy steps one at a time.

## Inputs you can use (not in git, absolute paths)
- Kokoro models: `lab/tts/models/kokoro-v1.0.int8.onnx` and `voices-v1.0.bin` (reference them, do not copy). The old venv no longer exists: create `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/production/.venv` (git-ignored) and `pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile` (small PyPI packages, say that you are doing it). See `lab/tts/tts.py` for the working call pattern.
- Whisper: `/opt/homebrew/bin/whisper-cli` with `lab/ffmpeg/models/ggml-base.en.bin`. FFmpeg is in `/opt/homebrew/bin` (no libass/freetype, not needed here).

## Build (minimal plain scripts, no frameworks)
1. **Validate the dialogue** (`validate.py`): speakers in {claude, human}, emotions in `emotionsAllowed`, non-empty text, `intro` shots well-formed; clear error messages.
2. **Synthesise each line** with its speaker's voice and speed (Claude `bf_emma`, human `am_michael`; check the voice names exist in the voices file and report if not), applying the `pronunciation` map (whole words). Save per-line WAVs (git-ignored).
3. **Word timing:** Whisper word timestamps for every line (as in the earlier labs) so each word has a start/end; match to the script words and report any mismatch. Per-line start/end are what the cuts use.
4. **Master timeline at 24 fps** (`timeline.json`, committed, strictly structured, documented in `production/README.md`):
   - Build the absolute schedule: `intro` shots first (durations from the dialogue file), then the lines with `gapBetweenLinesSec` between them, a 0.5 s lead-in before line 1 and a 1.0 s tail after the last line.
   - `shots`: ordered list `{id, shot, camera, startFrame, endFrame}`: the intro shots, then interior cuts by the `cutRule` (Claude speaks -> `CAM_HUMAN_POV`, human speaks -> `CAM_CLAUDE_POV`; cut on the first word; the gap stays on the previous shot; **minimum hold 1.2 s**: if a line is shorter, or two lines of the same speaker follow, merge or hold sensibly and say what you did). Frames are inclusive/exclusive as documented; no gaps or overlaps; first frame is 1.
   - `lines`: per line `{id, speaker, emotion, text, startFrame, endFrame, words:[{w,startFrame,endFrame}]}`.
   - `emotionKeys`: per speaker a list of `{frame, emotion, intensity}` (set at line start with a short lead-in of about 4 frames; return to `neutral` after the line ends; the listener reacts: for each line, the other character gets a small reaction key, e.g. `curious` or `amused`, about 6 frames after the line starts; keep it simple and documented).
   - `envelopes`: per speaker an array with one value (0..1) per frame for the whole video (RMS envelope of the speaker's own voice, smoothed with fast attack/slow release, silence = 0). Save as separate files `envelope_claude.json`, `envelope_human.json` (same length as the video).
   - `phone`: the typing animation timing for the text in the phone shot (`phoneText`, start frame, characters per second about 12, send frame).
   - `motionCues`: named frames derived from the intro: `human_exit_start`, `human_at_curb`, `car_arrive_start`, `car_stopped`, `door_closed` (about 0.8 s after the pickup shot ends is fine: it is a cut), `drive_start` (start of the interior cuts), `end`. State your assumptions (car approach takes about 3 s in the pickup shot).
   - `meta`: fps 24, total frames, total seconds, voices, versions of tools.
5. **Audio mix** (`mix.sh` or a small script): dialogue placed on the timeline (sample accurate to the frame times), plus a very quiet generated ambience made only with FFmpeg synthesis (no downloads, no copyrighted music): city/road noise (filtered brown noise) for the whole video, a slightly louder car-arrival swell in the pickup shot, and a low engine hum during the interior (a low filtered saw/sine mix with a tiny wobble), all well below the voices and gently ducked under speech (`sidechaincompress` ratio 3-4). Two-pass loudnorm to about -14 LUFS (verify with ebur128; target -14.0 +/- 0.3 and true peak <= -1.0 dBTP). Outputs (git-ignored large files): `out/audio_mix.wav`, `out/audio_voices_only.wav`.
6. **Checks** (`check.py`, runs from the timeline): total duration of audio matches the timeline frames within 1 frame; shots tile the whole range; every line lies inside exactly one shot; minimum hold 1.2 s respected; envelope lengths match; emotion names valid. Print a one-page summary table: per line start/end seconds, duration, words per minute, shot; total length; the cut list.
