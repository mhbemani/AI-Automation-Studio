# Agent brief: "car-render" — animate the car scene from the timeline and render the first sample video

Goal of this round: **one complete sample video** of the car scene (about 55.6 s, 24 fps), animated from the prepared timeline, with the mixed audio, **plus honest numbers on how heavy the render is on this Mac** (per-frame time per shot, total time, memory, disk). Quality target: "clearly a proper first sample" (clean cuts, readable emotion, no glitches), not final polish. Characters, car and voices are placeholders that will be replaced later: build the animation so replacing them is easy, and document how.

## Read first (in this order)
`lab/BRIEF.md`, `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/AGENT.md` (live Blender bridge `bridge/bl.py`, working rules, conversation style: all still apply), `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/SCENE_NOTES.md` (scene objects, rebuild order, cameras, ring road, `drive_car.py`, `set_aspect.py`), `lab/results/car-scene-fix.md`, `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/production/README.md` (schema of `timeline.json`), `lab/results/car-audio-timeline.md`, `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/production/dialogue_v1.json`. Apple M2, **8 GB RAM**: stay light, run heavy steps one at a time, never run two Blender renders at once.

## Getting the working files (not in git)
Copy from the base checkout (absolute paths; copy, do not move) into your worktree's `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/`: `scene.blend`, `assets/`, and into `production/out/`: `audio_mix.wav`, `audio_voices_only.wav`. All under `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/`. A Blender window from an earlier agent may still be open on the old file (with the old bridge): **never kill it**; ask the creator to save and close it, then launch your own with `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/launch_blender.sh lab/scene-lowpoly-car/scene.blend`. Final handoff: copy your final `scene.blend` and the sample MP4 to the same base-checkout paths if the harness allows, otherwise give the exact paths in the report.

## Part 1: Animation (script-driven, from `timeline.json`, live in Blender so the creator can watch)
Write one idempotent, deterministic script `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/scripts/animate.py` (plus small helpers if needed) that builds all animation **from `production/timeline.json` and the envelope files** and can be re-run after any scene change (own what you create via `cs_owner`, respect `user_locked`). Re-running the whole chain must be documented: `build_*` scripts, `drive_car`, then `animate`. Frame range 1..1335 at 24 fps (use the timeline's `meta`). Work in this order, looking at snapshots (`bl.py snap`) after each step:
1. **Shots and cuts:** timeline markers bound to the cameras exactly at the timeline's shot frames (intro EST, PHONE, EST, then the interior cuts: Claude speaks -> `CAM_HUMAN_POV`, human speaks -> `CAM_CLAUDE_POV`). At each interior cut keyframe the mirror aim `CAR_RearViewMirror["aim_<camera>"]` with constant interpolation (see the notes: a wrong mirror aim means no face in the mirror). Verify by snapping the first and last frame of every shot.
2. **Intro action:** the human walks out of the building door and stops at the curb (`human_exit_start` 13 -> `human_at_curb` 73; use the shipped Walk action from the human rig, then the standing/texting pose; foot sliding is acceptable but keep it plausible, adjust speed/path so the feet roughly match). In the PHONE shot the message "Claude, I wanna go home." is typed on the phone screen (`phone` block of the timeline: start/typing rate/send frame; e.g. per-character visibility keys; it must render deterministically in headless background mode, no add-ons).
3. **Car:** the car must be somewhere behind the pickup point out of shot at the start, approach and **stop at the curb exactly where the human waits** (`car_arrive_start` 181 -> `car_stopped` 253, ease out), wait (the human "gets in" off screen during the cut: the seated human only exists in the interior shots, check this cannot be seen in the EST shot before the cut), then from `drive_start` (277) accelerate smoothly to cruise speed (about 8.5 m/s) and lap the closed ring (the existing `drive_car.py` constraint, update it so the approach, stop and departure curve are keyed; no abrupt 0 -> 8.5 m/s). Cruise at constant speed to the end: in about 44 s the car covers about 375 m, which includes the first corner (about 167 m past the pickup): the interior cameras must show a natural turn. Wheels spin proportionally to the distance travelled (driver expression); the steering wheel and Claude's arms turn with the path curvature.
4. **Claude (body and eye emotions, no mouth):** from `emotionKeys` and `envelopes`: eye states (blink at random times with a fixed seed, wide for surprised/excited, curved/squinted for happy/proud via scale, looking slightly toward the mirror/road), body language (lean, tilt, small squash/stretch) per emotion, and **speech reaction driven by the speech envelope**: while Claude speaks its body bobs/pulses with the loudness (subtle, not wobbly), it settles in silence. Claude keeps both hands on the steering wheel; head/body keep eyes on the road, with quick glances to the mirror when the human speaks. Make emotions readable in stills: render a contact sheet of every emotion (`happy, curious, excited, surprised, thoughtful, proud, amused, neutral`) from CAM_HUMAN_POV and fix weak ones.
5. **Human (seated):** subtle idle (breathing, small head sway), head tilts/nods and posture changes from his `emotionKeys` and his own envelope (listening reactions when Claude speaks: small nod, tilt; he has no mouth animation: his face is a texture, so speech reads through head/body motion only). Keep hands in a believable rest pose (fix the earlier "hands hang at the sides/knees close to the seat" issues if cheap).
6. **Camera feel:** a very small handheld-style drift (low-frequency noise, fixed seed) on the two interior cameras only, so the shots do not feel nailed to the car; keep the mirror/face framing valid (verify with snaps). The EST cameras stay static.
7. **Verification of the animation (numeric where possible):** a script/report checking that the shot markers match the timeline exactly (frame by frame), the car is stationary and at the curb during frames 253-277 and within 0.3 m of the human's position, the speed profile is smooth (no jumps), the mirror aim keys exist at every interior cut, and a lap-position sanity check at the last frame. Snap one frame per shot (all 14) and look at each; fix flaws (clipping, characters out of frame, mirror empty, flicker-prone things).

## Part 2: Render and assemble (measure everything)
1. **Render script** `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/scripts/render_sequence.py` run headless (`Blender -b scene.blend -P ... -- <options>`): render frame ranges to PNG sequences (RGBA not needed), options `--preset draft|final`, `--range a b`, `--shots S04,S05`, `--every N`, resume-friendly (skip frames that exist, so a crash or stop loses little), deterministic noise (fixed seed), one Blender at a time. **Close or avoid the GUI Blender during long renders**: GUI plus renderer on 8 GB is too much: ask the creator to close the GUI window first (never kill it), and say when you will reopen it.
2. **Presets:** `draft` = 960x540; `final` = 1920x1080. Find the cheapest Eevee settings (samples, shadows, light threshold, denoise if available, raytracing resolution scale, planar probe resolution/refresh) that still look clean for each preset: test on 3 representative frames (an exterior EST, an interior HUMAN_POV with the mirror, an interior CLAUDE_POV with the mirror) with a short table of time/quality. Look at the images, state the quality loss honestly, and keep the mirror faces crisp.
3. **Measurements (the creator wants to know how heavy this is):** with `/usr/bin/time -l` (peak RSS and footprint) and wall clock, record: time per frame for every one of the 14 shots (sample at least 8 spread frames per shot at the chosen presets: the shots differ a lot: exterior vs. interior with the planar probe), total estimated time for 1335 frames at draft and at final, the actual measured total for the full draft render, peak memory of the render process, memory pressure/swap (check `vm_stat` before/after), CPU/GPU utilisation notes, disk used per frame and per sequence, temperature/throttling if visible (frame times drifting upward during the long run: report the drift). Also estimate the cost of the final preset at 24 fps vs. alternatives (e.g. render the EST/PHONE shots at 24 fps and static-camera segments less often, adaptive sampling): list options and expected savings, do not implement them unless trivial.
4. **Full draft render:** render **all 1335 frames at the draft preset** (sequentially, in the background, with resume). While it runs do not start other heavy work; check progress periodically with short `ls`/`tail` commands (do not poll in a tight loop). If frame times show it will take longer than about 2 hours, stop, report, and propose a cheaper preset instead of burning hours.
5. **Assemble:** FFmpeg: PNG sequence -> H.264 (yuv420p, 24 fps, CRF ~18, faststart) muxed with `production/out/audio_mix.wav` (AAC 192k): `production/out/sample_draft.mp4` (git-ignored). Verify: duration equals the audio within 1 frame, frame count 1335, `ffprobe` output saved, loudness unchanged. Also produce a contact sheet (one frame every ~2 s) and look at it; look at 3 consecutive-frame strips around two cuts to confirm clean cuts.
6. **Final-quality sample (optional, decide with the numbers):** do NOT render the full video at 1080p unless the measured estimate is under about 3 hours; instead render a **10-second 1080p excerpt** (one interior exchange across a cut, e.g. lines 2-3) and the three representative single frames at final settings, so the creator can compare draft vs final quality, and report the full-final estimate.

End with specific recommendations on how to cut render cost and on what to improve next. Commit scripts, notes and small stills/contact sheets on your branch (commit messages follow `docs/GIT_RULES.md`); frames and videos are git-ignored.
