# Agent brief: "character-selection" — find, test and shortlist a better human character (cheap exploration pass, no big build)

The creator does not like the human characters we have so far (the 66K-tri BlenderKit "Oscar" was too heavy; the 3.2K-tri cut-up scan was crude; the 6K-tri from-scratch rigged one built by the `human-rig` lab, in `lab/human-rig/`, is technically good but the creator does not like how it looks). Before anyone spends another big effort on building a character, this agent runs a **cheap, careful selection stage**: find good candidates, test them in the real scene under the real lighting and cameras, compare them fairly, and let the creator choose. **You do not build or rig a character from scratch and you do not integrate anything into the scene.** You research, test and report.

## Read first
`lab/BRIEF.md` (project rules: $0, minimal, ask before big downloads, never `pkill` things you did not start); `lab/results/scene-polycount-comparison.md`, `lab/results/realistic-scene-diet.md`, `lab/results/human-rig.md` (what exists, budgets, how the human is used: front passenger seat in a yellow 1970s convertible, phone-texting opening shot, 8 emotions, speech-driven mouth, Rigify control names); `lab/scene-realistic-car/SCENE_NOTES.md`, `ASSETS.md`; `lab/tools/polycount.py`. The scene and the labs are **read-only** for you: copy `lab/scene-realistic-car/scene.blend` (84 MB original is archived; use the current ~30 MB `scene.blend`) into your own folder for testing.

## Step 0: find out what the creator actually wants (ask first, briefly)
Start the conversation by asking the creator (one or two short questions, with the options below as examples): **what exactly do they dislike** about the current characters (face? faceted look? proportions? hands? clothes?) and **what look they want** (e.g. stylised animated-film like Pixar/Arcane-lite, game-style like GTA/Fortnite-ish low poly, cel-shaded, realistic-lite). Ask for 1-3 reference images or links (you can look at images they paste or save to a folder) or a few words per reference. Do not guess the target look. Record the answer as the **criteria** in your report. The existing constraints still hold: seated in the car at about 70 px face height in the two-shot and about 155 px full-figure in the opening shot, so faces must read at small sizes; the human must be rigged for sitting, phone texting with finger movement, head turning, and facial expressions (or have face shape keys); budget at most about 12K tris (8K preferred) and 1K-2K textures; the creator's M2 has 8 GB, so no heavy meshes.

## Allowed sources and rules
- **BlenderKit** (the creator is logged in; free assets only, check each asset's licence on its page: Royalty Free is OK, CC0/CC-BY OK with credit). You never handle the login: if the add-on or the site needs it, ask the creator to log in or to download a batch of asset IDs for you; import is done by copying from `~/blenderkit_data/models` (the add-on's own import step stalls, see `lab/results/realistic-scene.md`).
- **CC0 / free libraries** with no account: Quaternius, Kenney, Poly Haven, OpenGameArt (check each licence), Blender Studio/Foundation open assets (CC0/CC-BY), MakeHuman output (CC0) if it can be used without an account.
- **Mixamo** needs an Adobe account: do not use it unless the creator explicitly says they have one and want it (ask once, as an option).
- **Not allowed:** Sketchfab (login), anything paid, anything with unclear/non-commercial licence, AI-generated models. Licence must allow modification and use in monetised videos; record name, author, URL, licence and credit text in `ASSETS.md`.
- **Ask before every download** (names, URLs, sizes, licences, why): batch the asks, keep it to small files; at most about 300 MB in total. Downloaded files go to `lab/character-selection/assets/` (git-ignored); commit only notes, small stills, scripts.

## Step 1: shortlist (research)
Search for **10-15 candidate humans** that fit the criteria. For each, collect: name, source, URL, licence, author, triangle/face count (note: BlenderKit's listed counts are faces, not tris), rig type (Rigify / Mixamo-compatible / game-style humanoid / none), whether it has fingers, a face rig or shape keys, textures (sizes), clothes separable from the body or fused, how it looks (preview image), and risks. Present a table (including the three existing characters as baselines: old Oscar, the 3.2K scan, the 6K from-scratch one) and recommend which **4-6 finalists** to test, with reasons. Ask the creator which to download.

## Step 2: test the finalists fairly (in the real scene, draft quality, headless)
For each downloaded finalist, in a copy of the scene (`lab/character-selection/scene_test.blend`; never touch the originals): import once, scale to 1.75 m, put it in the front passenger seat pose (a simple pose is fine: rotate the thigh/knee bones or use the asset's own sit pose; if it cannot sit, note that) and in a standing pose on the sidewalk, and render with the **same cameras and lighting as the scene**, draft 960x540, 16 samples, headless, one at a time (8 GB: never two Blender renders at once; keep the GUI closed unless the creator wants to watch): `CAM_EST_Street` (check the face/figure at about 155 px height), `CAM_INT_TwoShot`, `CAM_CLAUDE_POV`, `CAM_PHONE_CU` (hand/fingers on the phone, if the asset has fingers), plus a front/side/3-4 turntable sheet and a face close-up. Make **one comparison sheet per camera** (candidates side by side with the existing characters, labelled) and one overall sheet. **Look at every image yourself** and judge honestly: does the face read at small size, how do the clothes and hands look, does it fit the golden-hour light, does it clash with Claude (the orange squarish character) and the car.
Also measure: triangles (`lab/tools/polycount.py` method, per part), texture sizes and the peak memory of one draft render (`/usr/bin/time -l`), and per-frame draft render time. For each finalist, a **rig check**: bone count/names, what a mapping to the scene's Rigify control names would take (our animation scripts need `torso, spine_fk..., neck, head, shoulder.*, upper_arm_fk/forearm_fk/hand_fk.*, thigh_fk/shin_fk/foot_fk.*, DEF-hand.R`, face shape keys incl. `MouthOpen`, `Blink` and the 8 emotions), can fingers curl, can it sit without clothing collapse, does the hood/collar/clothing deform acceptably when the head turns and arms raise (try 3 quick test poses and show them). **Do not fix, retopologise or customise anything**: only estimate what customisation (colours, eyes, mouth, hands, clothes) and re-rigging each candidate would need, in hours of work and risk.

## Step 3: report and let the creator choose
`lab/results/character-selection.md`: the creator's criteria; the full candidate table; the finalists' comparison sheets (paths); an honest ranking with pros/cons; for each finalist: licence, tris, rig compatibility, estimated customisation/integration effort, risks; what would make the current from-scratch character acceptable if the creator prefers to improve it (be specific about what you saw wrong: face, hands, proportions); your recommendation of 2-3 options (including "improve the current one" as an option), and what the next agent would need to do for each. Then **ask the creator to pick** and stop. Commit notes/stills/scripts on your branch (commit messages follow `docs/GIT_RULES.md`), `.blend` files and downloaded assets stay out of git (add ignore rules for `lab/character-selection/assets/`, `scene_test.blend`, `*.blend1`, `previews/`, `backups/` if missing).

## Conversation style
Short messages; one or two questions at a time; recommendations with reasons; show the sheets by path and describe what you see; never claim something looks good without having looked at the image. Be frank about what you cannot judge (motion, real-time feel, the creator's taste: that is why you ask).

## Don'ts
Do not build, rig, retopologise or customise a character; do not integrate anything into the scene or edit `lab/scene-realistic-car`, `lab/scene-lowpoly-car`, `lab/human-rig`; no accounts, no paid or unclear-licence assets, no AI-generated imagery, no add-ons, no `pkill`, no background processes left running when you finish.
