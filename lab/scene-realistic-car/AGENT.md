# Agent brief: "realistic-car-scene" — build a second, more realistic version of the car scene, live in Blender with the creator

You are a **hands-on 3D scene agent and guide**. You work WITH the creator in a conversation, inside a Blender window that stays open on their screen; they watch every change and may edit the scene themselves at any time. Your job: build a **second scene, `lab/scene-realistic-car/`, with a more realistic look and feel**, for the same story and the same shots as the finished stylised sample, and prove it works.

## Context you must read first
1. `lab/BRIEF.md` (project rules: $0, no accounts, minimal, ask before big downloads, never `pkill` things you did not start).
2. `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/AGENT.md` (**the live Blender bridge, working rules for a scene the creator also edits, conversation style: all still apply to you**), `SCENE_NOTES.md`, `ASSETS.md`, `SCENE_FIX_SPEC.md`, `production/README.md`, `production/dialogue_v1.json`, `production/timeline.json`.
3. `lab/results/car-scene-fix.md`, `car-audio-timeline.md`, `car-render.md` (what was learned: the mirror solution, the swap contract, render timings, known visual flaws).
The finished stylised scene lives in `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/` and is **read-only for you** (it is the creator's sample: never modify it, never delete it, never kill a Blender session showing it). Copy scripts, the bridge and ideas from it into your own folder.

## The story and shots (identical to the stylised scene)
A human walks out of a building and texts Claude "Claude, I wanna go home."; Claude (the custom orange, squarish octopus-like character, driving the car) picks him up; the main scene is inside the car: Claude in the **left front seat** (left-hand drive), the human in the **back right seat**, talking while Claude drives through the city. Cameras (same names): `CAM_EST_Street`, `CAM_PHONE_CU`, `CAM_HUMAN_POV` (back-right seat: Claude's right side/back and **Claude's face in the rear-view mirror**), `CAM_CLAUDE_POV` (driver: road through the windshield and **the human in the mirror**). The cut follows who is speaking (see the timeline's shots). The car drives a **closed, squarish ring road** (long straights, rounded corners) so it can lap forever. **No animation work in this task** (only static poses and a driveable path); the animate/render step comes afterwards.

## What "more realistic" means here (the creator's brief)
A believable, grounded, cinematic look instead of toy low-poly: real-world scale and proportions (car about 4.5 m, human about 1.75 m, lane widths, curbs, sidewalks), **PBR materials** (asphalt with lane markings and wear, concrete, brick/plaster/glass facades, painted metal, glass, rubber), real-looking street furniture (lamp posts, traffic lights, signs, hydrants, benches, bins, trees, parked cars, crosswalks), bevelled edges, HDRI sky lighting with believable sun, soft shadows, a filmic colour transform, subtle depth of field on the exterior shots, a real-looking car with a real interior, and a human that looks like a person (still simple and stylisation-tolerant). Claude stays Claude (same custom character, same orange identity), but re-shaded for this world (rounded bevels, soft material, subtle subsurface/specular) so it fits. The creator accepts "realistic-leaning" if a truly photoreal result is impossible on this Mac: be honest about the trade-offs, show stills, and let the creator choose.

## Hard constraints
- **Apple M2, 8 GB RAM.** Eevee Next (no Cycles for the sequence); textures at most 2K (1K for far/small items), instanced geometry (linked duplicates/collection instances), render-visibility/culling for far geometry, no 4K, no heavy displacement or hair. Target frame times (measure and report): about 3-4 s at 960x540 and about 8-10 s at 1080p for interior shots with the mirror, Blender peak under about 2.5 GB. If the realistic look exceeds that, reduce what the cameras see before reducing the story, and tell the creator what you traded.
- **$0, no accounts, no card, no add-ons.** Allowed sources only if you verify the licence on the asset's own page and record it: **Poly Haven** (CC0 models, textures, HDRIs; has a public API), **ambientCG** (CC0 PBR textures/HDRIs), Blender Foundation/Studio assets that are free to download without a login and CC0/CC-BY (CC-BY needs an attribution line in the credits, which you record), CC0 libraries such as Kenney/Quaternius only for items with no better realistic option, and **MakeHuman** (free open-source tool; its exported characters are CC0) if you can install and use it without an account. **Not allowed:** Sketchfab, BlenderKit, Mixamo, Turbosquid and anything that needs a login or purchase. Use web search to find the best qualifying assets for: a realistic car **with an interior if possible**, a realistic rigged human, street furniture, building facade textures or modular buildings, road/asphalt textures, HDRIs. If a good car with interior does not exist under these rules, propose the options (e.g. a good exterior plus an interior you build with PBR materials, as in the stylised scene) with preview images, recommend one, and let the creator pick.
- **Ask before every download** (name, URL, size, licence, why). Batch related files into one ask. Downloads go to `lab/scene-realistic-car/assets/` (git-ignored); record everything in `lab/scene-realistic-car/ASSETS.md` (name, URL, licence, author/credit text, date, size, local file) so it is reproducible and attributable.
- Work only inside `lab/scene-realistic-car/` (never edit `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/`, other labs, n8n/Docker). Commit scripts/notes/small stills on your branch after each milestone (commit messages follow `docs/GIT_RULES.md`); `scene.blend` and assets stay out of git (git-ignore rules already exist for them).

## Reuse what already works (do not re-solve solved problems)
- Copy `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/bridge/` and `launch_blender.sh` into your folder (the token is created on first launch, git-ignored) and drive Blender through `bridge/bl.py` exactly as before. Open it first, keep it open, never kill or quit a Blender you did not start. A Blender window from earlier agents may be open on the stylised scene: ask the creator to save and close it before you launch yours; do not close it yourself.
- Reuse the **rear-view mirror solution** (Eevee planar probe, per-shot aim, `hide_probe_plane` on mirror parts, ray tracing "Screen-Trace" at resolution scale 1) and the **ring-road and drive-path logic** from `scripts/build_city.py`, `drive_car.py` and `build_cameras.py` as starting points; adapt them rather than rewriting from scratch. Read `lab/results/car-render.md` section 3 (**swap contract**) carefully: **your scene must satisfy the same contract** (object names, pivots, parents, the `CAM_*` names, the `CAR_RearViewMirror` aim property, `CITY_DrivePath`, Claude's part names and pivots, the human armature bone names or a documented mapping) so that the existing `animate.py`, `drive_car.py`, `render_sequence.py` and `timeline.json` can drive your scene with as few changes as possible. Keep the same rebuild-script architecture (idempotent scripts owning their objects by `cs_owner`, respecting `user_locked`).
- Fix what the stylised sample did badly, from the start: (1) the opening shot's human was unreadable (about 50 px tall): frame `CAM_EST_Street` so the human is clearly readable (at least about 150 px tall at 960x540) while the car arrival is still visible; (2) `CAM_HUMAN_POV`: Claude's face must be clearly readable in the mirror (not two tiny slits): enlarge/reposition the mirror view or camera so the eyes and face shape read, with Claude's body at most about 35% of the frame width, no big blocks of car shell filling the frame; (3) the human should have a way to show expression (shape keys or swappable face textures) if the chosen human asset allows it, and visible hands in a believable rest pose; (4) the mirror must not show a blown-out white rear window.

## How you talk to the creator
As in `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/AGENT.md`: propose a short milestone plan and get a yes; **one clear step at a time, look at a snapshot after each meaningful change (Read the PNG: never claim it looks right unasked), say in 1-2 sentences what you did, ask one question or move on; offer 2-3 options with a recommendation when a real choice exists; teach just enough Blender (Outliner, G/R/S, N panel, numpad 0, Ctrl+Z) so the creator can edit by hand; check the scene state before every batch and never overwrite what the creator moved (`user_locked`).** Questions to settle early (one or two at a time, with a recommended default; "you decide" means use the default): the city feel (recommended: a generic modern mid-rise downtown street with shops and trees, not a specific real city), time of day and weather (recommended: late-afternoon golden hour with a slightly hazy sky for warm, flattering light; the stylised scene already uses golden hour, so also offer overcast or blue hour for variety), car type (recommended: a plain mid-size sedan with a visible roomy back seat), Claude's reshaded look, how realistic the human should be.

## Milestones
0. Open Blender with the bridge, verify with a snapshot, check free memory (n8n/Postgres containers from earlier labs may still be running, about 400 MB: **ask** before stopping them), agree the plan, ask the early questions.
1. **Asset research and plan:** web research under the rules above; present a short table (candidate, licence, size, preview image if you can fetch one, why, risk) for car, human, buildings/facades, roads, street furniture, vegetation, HDRIs; recommend; ask; download; fill ASSETS.md.
2. Import and organise (collections `01_CITY`, `02_CAR`, `03_CHARACTERS`, `04_CAMERAS`, `05_LIGHTS`, `06_PROPS`, `00_LIB`; real scale; naming per the contract; origins/pivots).
3. **City and closed ring road** (about 360 x 216 m centreline, 14-18 m corner radius, closed cyclic `CITY_DrivePath`; pickup point and building exit on a long straight, 167 m or more from a corner; buildings both sides of the whole ring, instanced with a fixed seed; street furniture, parked cars, trees, crosswalks; distant skyline/ground so the horizon is never empty). Prove one full lap with 10 sampled frames from `CAM_CLAUDE_POV` using `drive_car`.
4. **Car and interior** (seats incl. back right, dashboard, wheel, windows with believable glass, rear-view mirror; camera positions physically possible).
5. **Characters:** the human (seated in back right, standing at the exit door with a phone), Claude (reshaded, driver seat), the phone prop.
6. **Mirror:** adapt the solved approach; show stills of both mirror shots.
7. **Cameras and markers** as in the stylised scene (same names and shot structure, plus 9:16 variants via a `set_aspect.py` equivalent); verify each with a snapshot.
8. **Lighting, world and look-dev:** HDRI/sun, interior fill/rim, street lights and headlights, windows lit as appropriate, filmic view transform, subtle exterior depth of field, colour grade; tune Eevee for the 8 GB M2.
9. **Draft stills, measurements, contract test and handoff:** real Eevee renders (960x540 and one 1080p) of all four cameras and 4 interior frames at different lap positions; **time and memory per frame** (as a table next to the stylised numbers: stylised CAM_CLAUDE_POV 1.8 s draft / 7.3 s final); then **try the contract**: copy `lab/scene-lowpoly-car/scripts/animate.py` and `drive_car.py` into your scripts, run them against your scene, and report precisely which parts work unchanged, which fail and why, and fix the cheap ones. Write `lab/scene-realistic-car/SCENE_NOTES.md` (collections, objects, cameras, decisions, credits, known issues, rebuild order, measurements, what the animate/render step must change). Save `scene.blend` (backup first in `backups/`), commit, copy the final `scene.blend` and nothing else large to `lab/scene-realistic-car/scene.blend` if the harness allows (otherwise report the exact path), and write `lab/results/realistic-scene.md` with an honest comparison against the stylised scene: what looks better, what looks worse, what it costs, and what you could not verify.
After milestone 9, stop and report. Animation, voices, rendering the full video and asset customisation are the next steps and are **not** part of this brief.
